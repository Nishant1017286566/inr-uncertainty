# Results

Generated by `analyse.py`. The observational sections use the completed 97-setting grids of the original study (v1, v2, v3); section 3 uses the intervention runs in `results/`.

## 1 - What the aleatoric head is measuring

`A` is defined as the mean predicted variance and is intended to estimate the observation-noise variance `sigma^2`, which is known exactly here. Across the 97-setting grid:

| version | A spans | corr(log sigma^2, log A) | corr(log train_rmse^2, log A) | A as estimate of sigma^2 | within 1.5x of truth |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| v1 | 4.6e-05-2.9e-02 (637x) | **+0.391** | **+0.958** | 6.74x median | 18% |
| v2 | 4.6e-05-3.1e-02 (679x) | **+0.354** | **+0.933** | 6.28x median | 13% |
| v3 | 4.6e-05-3.1e-02 (679x) | **+0.354** | **+0.933** | 6.28x median | 13% |

In the 48 settings where the noise knob is at its baseline, so `sigma^2` is *identical by construction*, `A` still ranges 4.75e-05 to 2.33e-02 (**490x**).

The quantity `A` is supposed to estimate explains almost none of its variation. The training residual explains nearly all of it.


## 2 - One law behind the three sweeps

The Gaussian NLL term `0.5*(log v + r^2/v)` is minimised at `v = r^2`: the head's optimum is the squared residual **on the training data**. If that is what it reports, then `A/sigma^2` must be a power law in `train_rmse/sigma` with exponent 2, and all three sweeps are one phenomenon.

| version | fitted slope | 95% CI | R^2 | n |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| v1 | **1.923** | [1.84, 2.00] | **0.955** | 97 |
| v2 | **1.864** | [1.74, 1.98] | **0.932** | 97 |
| v3 | **1.864** | [1.74, 1.98] | **0.932** | 97 |

Theory says 2. One free parameter collapses 679x of variation in `A` onto a single line, in every version of the code.

### The three regimes

`train_rmse/sigma` says which side of the noise floor the fit sits on, and that alone predicts whether `A` is too low, right, or far too high.

| regime | train_rmse/sigma | settings | median A/sigma^2 | where it occurs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| memorising | <0.8 | 11 | **0.26x** | few images, or a wide net on little data |
| matched | 0.8-1.25 | 13 | **0.75x** | a narrow band only |
| underfitting | >1.25 | 73 | **9.78x** | many images, narrow nets, short training |

`A` is a usable noise estimate in 13 of 97 settings. Everywhere else it reports the model's own misfit, or its memorisation, as a property of the data.


## 3 - Causal test: two knobs that move the residual and nothing else

Pre-registered in `preregistration.md` before these ran. Baseline `n=10, width=50, noise=10`, 3 seeds, full fidelity.

**One pre-registered setting was dropped for a reason unrelated to the hypotheses.** `DIVERSITY = 4` is outside the domain of the study's own image family: the blob-width term `0.020*(1 + D*U(-0.3, 0.3))` turns negative for 8 of the 100 images at `D = 4`, which flips a Gaussian into a divergent exponential and produces non-finite pixels. The family is well defined only for `D < 3.33`, so `D = 4` was replaced by `D = 3`. This is a latent limit in the generator, not a result, and it would silently corrupt any future sweep of that knob.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0.99 | 1.168e-04 | 1.569e-04 | **0.74x** | 2.408e-05 |
| 0.5 | 2.17 | 2.451e-04 | 1.666e-04 | **1.47x** | 1.547e-04 |
| 1 | 2.34 | 4.114e-04 | 1.831e-04 | **2.25x** | 2.655e-04 |
| 2 | 2.16 | 5.491e-04 | 2.414e-04 | **2.27x** | 2.584e-04 |
| 3 | 1.94 | 6.746e-04 | 3.257e-04 | **2.07x** | 2.663e-04 |

Spearman rho(diversity, A) = **+0.982**, A range **6.4x**.

**H1 ACCEPTED** (needs rho > 0.7 and range > 2x). Raising task difficulty at identical capacity, data volume and noise level moves the noise estimate as the law requires.

### I2 - optimisation budget (STEPS)

Spearman rho(steps, A) = **-0.981**, A range **12.6x**.

**H2 ACCEPTED** (needs rho < -0.7). Training longer changes nothing about the data, the model or the noise, yet the reported aleatoric uncertainty falls — 12.6x across the budget range.

### H3 - the interventions obey the grid's law

Pooled over both interventions (33 runs): slope **1.939** [1.53, 2.48], R^2 **0.882**. The observational grid gave 1.864; theory says 2.

**H3 ACCEPTED** (needs slope in [1.4, 2.4] and R^2 > 0.80).

### H4 - is it the *training* residual, or just error in general?

| predictor of A | median multiplicative error |
|---|---|
| `sigma^2` (its claimed meaning) | 2.14x |
| `err^2` (error at held-out test points) | 2.74x |
| `train_rmse^2` (residual on training data) | **2.22x** |

Note against ourselves: on these 33 runs `sigma^2` is the best of the three (2.14x). That is expected and does not rescue it — the interventions sit at one point of the grid, so `sigma^2` barely varies and a constant is a decent fit. The grid is where that comparison is informative, and there `sigma^2` is +0.35 correlated with `A` against +0.93 for the residual. H4 asks only which of the two *error* quantities the head is reading.

**H4 ACCEPTED**. The training residual is the better predictor, which is what the NLL optimum predicts: the head sees training residuals, not test error.

### H5 (falsifier) - does the epistemic term obey the same law?

Pre-registered test, pooled over both interventions: regressing `log(E/sigma^2)` on `log(train_rmse/sigma)` gives slope **1.382**, R^2 **0.638**, against 1.939 / 0.882 for `A`.

**H5 REJECTED** (needs abs(slope) < 1.0 or R^2 < 0.5).

The falsifier fired: on the pooled runs `E` does trend with the training residual, so the pre-registered test does not establish that the effect is specific to the aleatoric head.

#### Post-hoc (not pre-registered): the pooled test conflates two interventions

`I2` changes only the optimisation budget, so the data, the model and the true epistemic problem are all fixed. `I1` changes the images themselves, which genuinely changes how much there is to disagree about — and also moves `sigma^2`, since the noise knob is a percentage of image standard deviation. Only `I2` isolates the head. Splitting the same regression:

| runs | A: slope, R^2 | E: slope, R^2 | A range | E range |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **I2** | 2.64, 0.987 | 0.14, 0.294 | 12.6x | 1.4x |
| **I1** | 1.21, 0.827 | 2.43, 0.970 | 6.4x | 12.3x |

Under `I2` alone, `A` moves **12.6x** and `E` moves **1.4x**. Training 27x longer leaves the epistemic term essentially where it was and cuts the aleatoric term by an order of magnitude. That is the asymmetry `M1` predicts, and it is what the pooled test hid.

Stated plainly: the pre-registered falsifier failed, and a post-hoc split recovers the prediction. That is weaker evidence than a pre-registered pass, and it is reported as such — the pooled test was the wrong test to have written down, because `I1` was never a clean probe of the aleatoric head alone.


## 6 - What it costs downstream

`rho_std_err` is the rank correlation between the predicted total standard deviation and the true absolute error, per setting: does the uncertainty point at the mistakes?

- across the grid it runs +0.03 to +0.85, median +0.31
- lowest: n=10, width=50, noise=100 gives **+0.026** — no better than chance
- it is **never negative** in any of the 96 settings (min +0.026), so the uncertainty degrades to uninformative rather than inverting. An earlier draft of this analysis claimed an inversion at width 1; that was a conflation of two different recorded correlations and is corrected here.
- correlation between the noise knob and `rho_std_err`: **-0.64** — the ranking degrades as noise grows, from +0.52 at 1% noise to +0.03 at 100%

A different correlation in the same study records whether the two terms move together across points within a setting — whether the split is even stable:

- `rho_rank(A, E)` runs -1.000 to +0.726, median **+0.457** across 96 settings
- at n=1, width=1, noise=10 it is **-1.000**: the two terms are almost perfectly *anti*-correlated, so whichever way the total moves, the attribution between 'noise' and 'ignorance' flips with it
- a median of about +0.46 means that, in the typical setting, the points the model calls noisy are also the points it calls uncertain — the split is not separating the two things it names
